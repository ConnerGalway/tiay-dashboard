Hotels raised rates rather than filling rooms

78%occupancy, May to Sept
$260average room rate
+14%rate vs summer 2025

Hotel occupancy slipped two points from last summer while the average room rate climbed 14%. This season's hotel growth came from rate, not from more rooms sold.

Short-term rentals moved differently. Listings, occupancy and rates all rose, and rental revenue grew by about a quarter. The two accommodation markets are growing in different ways.

Who this speaks to: a panel of roughly 600 hotel rooms, concentrated in Whitehorse. It does not describe smaller or seasonal properties elsewhere in the territory.

What people are searching for

The only forward-looking panel on the page. Search interest signals the direction of demand. It is not a projection of visitors or revenue.

Which markets are searching for the Yukon

▲ 12%search interest, Sept to Oct vs last year

What it meansInterest in Yukon travel is running ahead of last autumn in Canada and the United States, and slightly behind in Germany. The direction is up; the size of any visitor change is not something search data can tell us.

Who it speaks toOperators planning for the winter and next summer. Search interest is an index relative to each market's own peak, not a count of people.

Which activities are searched for most

Dog sleddingfastest-growing activity search

What it meansDog sledding searches grew fastest and hiking and paddling rose too, while northern lights searches softened. The mix of what people look for is shifting toward active experiences.

Aurora interest tracks the solar cycle, which peaked in 2024 and 2025. Softening interest may have nothing to do with the Yukon.

Who it speaks toTour and activity operators. Activity searches are small samples compared with destination searches, so month-to-month moves can be noisy.
